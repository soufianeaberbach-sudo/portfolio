Process

Five stages.
No surprises.

Every piece goes through the same five. You get a written output at the end of each one, so there is never a stage where you don't know what is happening.

Stage 01

Sketch

Concept locked before cost

Silhouette, fabric direction and the constraints that will actually govern the piece — agreed in writing before anything is cut. Most failures downstream trace to a decision skipped here.

Typical: 2–4 days
Stage 02

2D Pattern

Geometry, not guesswork

Drafted in Gerber, Lectra or Optitex to your block, your tolerances, your seam allowances. Not scaled from a sample, not traced from a garment — drafted.

Typical: 3–6 days
Stage 03

Gradation

The size run that holds

Full nest checked against your measurement chart at every size. Grade rules written down, so the same result is reproducible next season by someone who is not me.

Typical: 2–3 days
Stage 04

3D Simulation

Fit resolved before it costs money

Drape, tension and fit resolved on screen. A fitting round caught here costs an afternoon; caught after sampling it costs a week and a courier.

Typical: 2–4 days
Stage 05

Final Garment

Spec the floor can follow

Construction, trims, stitch types, marker and consumption. Everything the factory needs so nobody on the line has to guess or phone you.

Typical: 3–5 days
Why it matters

A factory rarely fails
at the sewing.

It fails at the pattern. Every extra fitting round, every point of fabric waste, every delayed sample traces back to a decision made before a single metre was cut. The five stages exist so that decision is made once, in writing, by someone who has done it 8,500 times.
